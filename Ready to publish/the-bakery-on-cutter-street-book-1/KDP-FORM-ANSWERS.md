## Description (paste as is)
Hannah Brooks came home to Cutter Street, the small Ohio town she left at eighteen, with one plan: sell her late grandmother's bakery and leave before the winter ends. The oven is dead, the back taxes are overdue, and the only person who can fix anything on the street is Eli Carver, the grumpy electrician who has kept the building running for years and has no intention of letting a city girl walk away with her grandmother's secrets. As they rebuild the ovens, the wiring and the trust between them, Hannah discovers a ledger hidden in a flour bin, a letter sealed in a hardware-store safe, and a promise her grandmother made to the whole street. Between Saturday markets, late nights in the kitchen, a developer's generous offer, and a town that refuses to sit quietly, Hannah must decide whether the life she is starting to love is worth more than the money on the table. Warm, funny and full of small-town heart, this is a sweet romance about second chances, rising bread, and the people who keep a street alive. No explicit content, no profanity, no on-page violence.
